This chapter connects ProSiT (Section~\ref{sec:prosit_background}) with the
results of Chapter~\ref{chp:results}. It shows how ProSiT is set up for the case,
which inputs it receives, what it learns from the training log, which settings
are used, and which extensions the physical limits of the terminal require. The
experiments that test these choices are described in Chapter~\ref{chp:experiments}.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{Inputs}
\label{sec:sim_overview}
\label{sec:sim_controlflow}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

ProSiT receives two inputs. A Petri net that describes which sequences of stops a
truck may perform, and the training log, from which it learns how often each path
is taken, where a stop takes place and how long trucks wait and are served. The
model is therefore a hybrid. It combines behaviour learned from the event log with
domain knowledge about the physical limits of the terminal that the log cannot
express (Sections~\ref{sec:rw_positioning} and~\ref{sec:sim_adaptations}).

\paragraph{The control flow.} The net is the Heuristics Miner net HM 0.7, selected
in E1 and E2 and used exactly as ProM discovered it (Figure~\ref{fig:sim_controlflow}).
It has 22 places, 11 visible transitions, one per activity, and 34 silent
transitions. The silent transitions come from the way ProM turns the heuristics net
into a Petri net \cite{Mannhardt2017iDHM}. Every dependency between two activities,
for example ``after Gate In, an RMG receive'', becomes one silent transition between
them. The decisions of
a visit, which stop comes next or whether the truck leaves, are therefore taken at
these silent transitions. The net has no parallel branches. A truck holds one token,
and its stops follow one another, as rule~4 requires. Its loops at RMG receive, RMG
delivery and HO2 receive allow two stops of the same kind in one visit. In the log,
such a visit is, for example, a truck that delivers two containers to two different
blocks. The block is the resource of a stop, not part of its activity label
(Section~\ref{sec:activities_order}), so both stops are recorded as RMG delivery.

\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/simulation_model/sim_control_flow.pdf}
\caption{Control flow of the simulation model, HM 0.7 as discovered in ProM (silent transitions as black bars).}
\label{fig:sim_controlflow}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{The event log.} The training log (Section~\ref{sec:cleaning_population})
gives every stop its start and complete time and its resource, the block for an RMG
stop, and every visit its case attributes (Table~\ref{tab:manifest_attributes}).

Three trees show what the model learns. They are typical for their parameters and
are read from the top to one leaf, as the arrival tree of

\paragraph{Routing.} Figure~\ref{fig:sim_tree_routing} shows the tree of the silent
transition from Gate In to an RMG receive. It splits only on case attributes. The
number $n$ in a node counts the training situations in which the transition was
enabled, once per visit in total (59,139). For a
receive visit with one planned stop and mostly full containers, the transition fires
with a probability of 0.88. For a delivery visit, practically never. The routing
trees thus reproduce which visit goes where first.

\paragraph{Waiting time.} One tree per resource predicts the time from the moment a

block. The expected waiting time grows from 7 minutes with at most one waiting truck

\paragraph{Execution time.} One tree per activity predicts how long a stop takes.
For RMG receives (Figure~\ref{fig:sim_tree_exec}), the first splits are on the number

\paragraph{Capacities.} ProSiT discovers the capacity of a resource from the
number of stops that overlap in the log (multitasking) \cite{Vinci2026prosit}. For LL and HO2 it finds 23
and 14 trucks at once. These areas record the whole stay of a truck, including
parking and waiting for handling, so the values describe how many trucks are in
the area rather than how many are served. For this study they are not critical, because
the analysis concerns the RMG blocks, and as long as the simulated stops at LL and
HO2 take as long as the recorded ones, their exact capacity does not affect the
blocks. For the RMG blocks, ProSiT discovers four or five trucks at once. The model
uses three, the number of handover lanes (Section~\ref{sec:sim_adaptations}).
Table~\ref{tab:sim_capacities} in Appendix~\ref{app:prosit_inventory} lists all
capacities and calendars.

\section{Settings}
\label{sec:sim_settings}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The discovery uses the hyperparameters of Section~\ref{sec:prosit_hyperparameters}
with the values of Table~\ref{tab:sim_hyperparameters}. Each value has a reason.

\input{tables/sim_hyperparameters}
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=2pt,topsep=4pt]
\item \textbf{Version 1.0.4.} It decides for every predictive parameter by cross-validation
whether a tree predicts better than one distribution for all cases
(Section~\ref{sec:prosit_trees}). Trees are kept where they help, so that waiting
and execution times can depend on the load and the attributes of a visit.
\item \textbf{Largest tree depth three.} The default is five. Trees of depth three
stay readable for the review with the terminal, and the cross-validation still
chooses the depth of each tree between one and three.
\item \textbf{Leaf sizes 50, 100 and 200.} This is the default list. Even the
smallest leaf holds enough visits for a stable distribution.
\item \textbf{Empirical attributes.} Every simulated truck receives a combination
of attributes that was observed, for example a planned number of stops that fits
its deliveries and receives. This matters, because the stop guard uses the planned
number of stops (Section~\ref{sec:sim_adaptations}).
\item \textbf{Workload features on.} The waiting at a block depends on how many
trucks are there and how many wait. Without these features, the waiting-time
trees could not react to the load at all.
\item \textbf{Multitasking on, threshold 5\,\%.} A block serves up to three trucks
at once. Without multitasking, every block would have a capacity of one. The
discovered capacities of the blocks are replaced by the three handover lanes
(Section~\ref{sec:sim_adaptations}).
\item \textbf{Case attributes.} The nine attributes of the planned visit
(Table~\ref{tab:manifest_attributes}), because they are known at Gate In.
\item \textbf{Random state 42.} The same inputs give the same model.
\end{itemize}

\section{Extensions for the Physical Limits}
\label{sec:sim_adaptations}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

ProSiT was developed and evaluated on event logs of business processes, in which
a resource typically handles one case at a time \cite{Vinci2026prosit}. A truck visit has physical limits that such a model does not know
(Section~\ref{sec:bg_terminal}). A truck performs the stops of its visit one after
another, and a block holds at most three trucks at once in its handover lanes. Five extensions add these
limits to the simulator (Table~\ref{tab:sim_adaptations}). Each is a switch in
ProSiT's simulator. With all switches off, it produces exactly the same log as
ProSiT (Appendix~\ref{app:model_inspection}). E3 and E4 test whether each extension
is needed (Chapter~\ref{chp:results}).

\paragraph{The stop guard.} The discovered net allows any number of stops, because
its loops can be repeated. In a data Petri net, the number of stops would be
controlled by guards. Conditions on case variables that a transition must satisfy
before it may fire \cite{Mannhardt2016Precision}. Two variables suffice for the
truck visit. The planned number of stops $n$, a case attribute of the pre-advised
visit that is known at Gate In (Section~\ref{sec:entry_known_attributes}), and the
number of stops done $c$, which every stop increases by one. A step towards another
stop then requires $c < n$, the step towards Gate Out $c = n$. With such guards,
soundness depends on the data. Every case must still be able to complete under
them \cite{FelliDeLeoniMontali2021}.

ProSiT builds on stochastic Petri nets whose transition weights depend on the data
of the case \cite{mannhardt2023stochasticProcessesModelling,Vinci2026prosit}. A
weight of zero would act like a guard, but ProSiT learns every weight from the log
and offers no way to state a condition \cite{Vinci2026prosit,ProSiTRepository}. Its
routing trees learn the planned number of stops only as a tendency. The stop guard
adds the condition to the simulator. Because the decisions of HM 0.7 sit on silent
transitions, it cannot wait until a stop or Gate Out is enabled. It looks ahead.
Before the simulation, it computes from the reachability graph of the net, for
every state and every value of $c$, the numbers of stops with which a visit can
still end. Before every routing draw, each enabled transition, silent or visible,
whose firing would make it impossible to end with exactly $n$ stops gets the
weight zero. The learned weights of the other transitions are kept and normalised,
and one transition is drawn. For example, a truck with one planned stop that has
finished its RMG stop can no longer take the silent transition back into a second
stop. Only the step towards Gate Out remains. In HM 0.7 every silent transition
leads to exactly one next activity, so the check amounts to the two guards above,
placed on the silent transitions. Because it is computed on the reachability graph,
no truck can enter a state from which it cannot complete its plan. The net with
these guards is sound with respect to the data. If all permitted transitions have a
learned weight of zero, the guard draws among the permitted ones.

\paragraph{Without the planned number of stops.} If the stops were not
pre-advised, the same mechanism could still enforce the physical bound of rule~6.
With $n$ replaced by the upper limit of eight stops and the condition $c \le 8$,
the guard would mask every step after which a visit would exceed the bound, and
the number of stops below it would be left to the learned routing, whose trees use
the activities done so far as features (Section~\ref{sec:prosit_components}). The
results of E3 indicate what this would give. Without any guard, the learned routing
already reproduces the distribution of stops per visit closely (total-variation
distance 0.0053 to the training log, Section~\ref{sec:res_baseline}). A bound would
remove only visits beyond it, and the observed maximum of four stops would be the
stricter bound to choose.

\paragraph{The other extensions.} The remaining four concern the blocks. Two of them
change the simulator and two change the training data of a predictive parameter.
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=2pt,topsep=4pt]
\item \textbf{Capacity at the start.} The capacity of three lanes is checked over
the whole stop when it starts, not only when it becomes possible. A stop that
finds all three lanes busy is moved to the first moment at which a lane is free for
its whole duration.
\item \textbf{Exact counting.} The number of trucks at a block is counted exactly.
\item \textbf{Waiting from a free lane.} In the training data of the waiting time,
two quantities are computed anew. The waiting is measured from the moment a lane of
the block became free, not from the moment the whole block was free, and the queue
length no longer counts the stop itself.
\item \textbf{Load in the execution time.} The training data of the execution time
receive one more feature, the number of other trucks at the block when the stop
starts.
\end{itemize}
The changed training data are computed from the training log only, and the trees
are learned with ProSiT's own discovery functions, hyperparameters and random
state. The installed ProSiT package is not changed. Appendix~\ref{app:implementation}
describes the implementation. Why each extension is needed follows from the
mechanism audit (Section~\ref{sec:res_mechanisms}).

\paragraph{Resource choice.} For an RMG stop, the resource is the block. ProSiT's
learned trees choose the block with the longer queue, because of the way its
training data count the queue (Section~\ref{sec:res_inspection}). In the simulation
this sends most trucks to one block. When the model replays the observed trucks,
it therefore takes the observed block of a stop wherever the simulated visit has an
observed counterpart for it, and the shares of the blocks in the training log
otherwise (24.7\,\% of the RMG stops). When it generates its own trucks, it
draws every block from these shares.

\section{Replay and Generative Mode}
\label{sec:sim_modes}
\label{sec:sim_execution}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The model can be run in two ways. In \emph{replay mode}, it simulates the trucks of
a recorded period. Every simulated truck arrives at the Gate-In time of one observed
truck and has its case attributes. The simulator then generates everything else,
namely the route, the waiting, the stops and the exit. Simulated and observed trucks can
thus be compared one by one, and an error in the demand cannot hide an error in the
model. This is how the model is validated on the test period. In \emph{generative
mode}, it creates its own trucks with the arrival tree and the observed combinations
of case attributes, as it would for a future period. Chapter~\ref{chp:results}
reports both.

In both modes the simulator handles the trucks in the order of time. It always
moves on with the truck whose next step comes first. For each step it determines
the possible next transitions, applies the routing trees and the stop guard, and
draws one. For a yard stop it chooses the block, the waiting time, the start (the
earliest time at which fewer than three trucks are at the block during the whole
stop) and the execution time. Table~\ref{tab:sim_worked_example} follows one
simulated truck in replay mode through these steps, with the values the simulator
used and the leaf of each tree that produced them.

\section{Chapter Summary}
\label{sec:sim_summary}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The simulation model combines the discovered net HM 0.7 with the decision trees
that ProSiT learns for arrivals, routing, resources, waiting and execution times,
and adds five extensions for the physical limits of the terminal. A stop guard for
the planned number of stops, three lanes per block, a waiting time measured from a
free lane, the load in the execution time and an exact count of the trucks at a
block. It runs in replay mode for validation and in generative mode for future
periods. Chapter~\ref{chp:results} reports how well it reproduces the terminal.
