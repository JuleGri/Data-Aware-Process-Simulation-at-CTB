\chapter{Results}
\label{chap:results}

This chapter presents the empirical findings of the CTB study. They reveal a
clear separation between structural conformance, historical timing fidelity,
and scenario responsiveness. The sequential Petri net covers almost all
held-out visits, whereas the simulator reproduces only parts of the observed
timing behavior and systematically underestimates truck turnaround. Adding
static or native workload state does not improve validation fidelity enough to
replace the parsimonious historical reference. Native queue state becomes
relevant for scenario behavior only under substantially increased demand, not
under the initial 20\,\% intervention. This distinction frames the interpretation
of all subsequent results.

The evidence is presented from model foundation to operational application.
The temporal partitions and control-flow analysis first establish the common
evaluation basis. The historical-fidelity and feature-source comparisons then
show which aspects of the later period are reproduced and which remain biased.
Analyses of temporal transfer, context, arrivals, resources, transition times,
and accumulated time burden locate the main explanatory boundaries without
claiming unobserved causes. The chapter closes with the T22-closure,
increased-demand, and saturation experiments, separating reproducible simulator
responses from physically validated terminal effects.

\section{Control-Flow Stability Across the Held-Out Period}
\label{sec:results_process_discovery}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The raw Inductive-Miner result is informative but is not sufficient as an executable CTB source model. On the 64\,\% discovery partition it contains
three parallel operators because frequently reversed sequential relations are
interpreted as concurrency. It also permits a complete yard bypass. Both
properties conflict with the terminal-expert case contract. The model used by all four ProSiT configurations is therefore the transparent repair defined in
Equation~\ref{eq:ctb_control_flow_contract}. It has 4 places, 12 transitions,
24 arcs, and no parallel operator. It requires at least one yard activity and
permits arbitrary sequential orders and repetitions. Table~\ref{tab:conformance_train_test}
reports the quality of this repaired source model after rediscovery of the
selected parameter configuration on the combined development period.

The test log contains 53 variants, ten of which are absent from development and
together account for ten of 17,892 cases (0.0559\,\%). Unlike a prefix trie,
the repaired loop can replay unseen sequential combinations. It fits all
17,892 test cases completely. This is substantive test evidence rather than a
consequence of memorising the construction log.

The remaining measures show the expected abstraction trade-off. Test precision
is 0.7641 because the loop permits more sequential combinations and
repetitions than were observed. This broader language is consistent with the
stated CTB contract because experts identified no forbidden yard successor
pair. Generalisation remains high at 0.9488. A hard operator check confirms
zero parallel cuts, and the ten final simulation replications produce neither
within-case overlap, gate-only cases, nor a \texttt{Gate Out} before the final
yard activity.

\medskip

\noindent\textbf{RQ2 answer.} Automatic discovery identifies a compact and
general control-flow language, but the raw result is not physically admissible
without domain repair. The expert-repaired source model achieves complete test
fitness, preserves sequential generalisation, and satisfies the mandatory
gate, yard-work, and no-concurrency conditions. The result supports the
repaired model as a simulation backbone. It does not support presenting the
unmodified Inductive-Miner output as a fully automatic operational model.

\subsection{Discovery-to-Execution Repair}

Transitioning from the raw tree to the executable tree is a primary modeling
step, rather than a secondary robustness test. It removes both forms of excess
behavior identified by the domain audit, consisting of the concurrent activities of one single truck
and the gate-only bypass. At the same time, it retains the activity alphabet
and deliberately allows arbitrary sequential yard combinations and
repetitions. The repair is fixed before validation and is identical for every
feature-source configuration. It therefore cannot explain performance
differences among the four ProSiT cells.

The raw tree itself remains an auditable discovery artifact. Its structure is
also sample-window sensitive, as the 64\,\% discovery tree contains three
parallel operators, when the tree rediscovered on the complete 80\,\%
development period contains none but still allows for the yard bypass. This
instability enhances the methodological finding that a high-fitness
discovered petri net is not automatically a physically valid simulation model.

\paragraph{Interpretation of the \texttt{LL\_mixed} Branch}

The special structural role of \texttt{LL\_mixed} is supported by a small and
highly concentrated set of visits. Across the complete train and hold-out
periods, 64 visits contain the activity and 57 of them contain no other yard
activity. Their observed pattern is therefore simply \texttt{Gate In},
\texttt{LL\_mixed}, and \texttt{Gate Out}. Only seven visits combine
\texttt{LL\_mixed} with another yard activity. In six training visits it occurs
first and is followed by \texttt{RMG\_receive}. The single held-out exception
follows the order \texttt{RMG\_delivery}, \texttt{LL\_mixed}. A plausible
operational interpretation is that an empty-container exchange is normally
completed before another yard area for routing reasons. The six training cases
are compatible with that explanation, but the small sample and held-out
exception do not justify a mandatory ordering rule. The isolated branch is
mainly evidence of an valid one-stop process variant rather than evidence
that \texttt{LL\_mixed} must always precede other yard work.

The rule audit explains why ordinary feature importance would be misleading.
The static-only bundle retains static-state splits on \texttt{target\_rank},
\texttt{target\_demand}, \texttt{rmg\_demand}, and
\texttt{mt\_utilization}. The combined bundle retains
\texttt{target\_rank\_group}, \texttt{target\_rank},
\texttt{target\_demand}, and \texttt{rmg\_demand}. Native-only and both each
retain one \texttt{queue\_length} split. Most retained rules nevertheless use
visit/process or location state. Correlation is concentrated within alternative static representations:
$\rho=0.922$ for gate and RMG utilisation, $0.898$ for gate and RMG demand, and
$-0.790$ for target utilisation and target rank. By contrast, the largest
absolute demand--native-state correlation is 0.190 and the largest
utilisation--native-state correlation is 0.019. Correlated attributes may
remain in an event log. The methodological issue is that attribution to an
individual split can depend on the candidate set, not that predictors must be
mutually independent.

The retained native rule also makes the white-box scope concrete. In the
\textit{native-only} and \textit{both} bundles, the waiting-time tree for T07
splits once at \texttt{queue\_length}$=4.5$. Values at or below the threshold
use a fitted exponential distribution with a reported node value of 6.825
minutes. Values above the threshold use a gamma distribution with a reported
node value of 27.474 minutes. This condition is inspectable and can be
challenged against domain knowledge. It is not treated as a causal queueing law,
and neither operational scenario edits this split or replaces one of its
subtrees. The scenarios demonstrate transparent parameter intervention, while
this retained rule illustrates the additional tree structure that ProSiT makes
available for future rule-level interventions.

The earlier semantically cleaned static sensitivity remains an exploratory
specification audit rather than a fifth selection cell. It motivated removal
of redundant aggregate, target-derived, binned and rank representations from
future model designs, but it is not used to choose the final model. The final
four-cell result is sufficient to conclude that the available workload state
does not provide a resolved validation improvement under the tested ProSiT
specification.

\paragraph{Historical reference-model decision}

\textit{Visit-only} is selected because none of the three additional-state
configurations passes the rule fixed before validation results were inspected.
It is therefore the parsimonious fallback, not a winner chosen by ranking noisy
point estimates. After this decision, \textit{visit-only} is rediscovered on
the combined 80\,\% development log and evaluated once on the untouched test
period. The final test results in Table~\ref{tab:final_kpis} do not participate
in this choice. This decision concerns RQ1 historical fidelity. It does not
remove the need to test whether a model used for RQ4 contains the endogenous
state required by an intervention.

\noindent\textbf{RQ3 answer.} Neither static workload proxies nor ProSiT's
native dynamic state produces a statistically resolved multi-perspective
validation improvement. More available state is therefore not automatically
more useful process state. Under the pre-specified selection rule, the evidence
favours the parsimonious \textit{visit-only} configuration for historical
reproduction of this event log. The demand-response adequacy audit in
Section~\ref{sec:results_scenarios} assigns a different, explicitly narrower
role to native dynamic state.

\paragraph{Meaning of the Time Measures}
\label{sec:results_temporal_note}

The real log has start and completion timestamps, but no enablement timestamp.
Service-time EMD uses $\text{complete}-\text{start}$. Turnaround EMD uses
$\text{last\_complete}-\text{first\_start}$. Both can be calculated for both
logs. ProSiT derives enablement internally and writes it only to the simulated
log. Simulated $\text{start}-\text{enabled}$ is called pre-service delay. It
cannot be validated against real waiting time.

\section{Temporal Transfer and Error Decomposition}
\label{sec:results_temporal_transfer}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The observed process changes between discovery and evaluation. Mean truck
turnaround rises from 37.293 minutes in training to 39.742 minutes in the
hold-out, a 2.450-minute increase (95\,\% Welch CI $[2.003,2.896]$).
The median rises from 29 to 32 minutes and P90 from 67 to 71 minutes. The
bootstrapped 95\,\% interval for the P90 increase is $[3,6]$ minutes. The
Wasserstein distance between the two real turnaround distributions is 3.235
minutes. The hold-out is therefore not an exchangeable sample from an
unchanged period.

The change is not confined to case composition. Mean service time increases
for all nine yard activities, with activity-specific increases between 1.20
and 4.01 minutes. Using held-out activity frequencies, their mean shift is
2.120 minutes and their aggregate Wasserstein distance is 2.131 minutes.
At the same time, process-flow composition changes by a total-variation
distance of 0.072 and target-area composition by 0.102. The receive share
falls by 7.22 percentage points.

This drift also affects a simple congestion interpretation of the available
state variables. Gate and RMG utilisation proxies are lower in the hold-out
(standardised shifts $-1.095$ and $-0.675$), while all yard-service means and
complete turnaround are higher. Several demand measures rise, but the
direction of the utilisation proxies is inconsistent with treating them as a
stable causal measurement of terminal congestion. They stay predictive
features whose relationships transfer empirically, not mechanical queue
state. This finding is consistent with recent work on online simulation
discovery, which gives greater weight to recent data while retaining historical
information to handle evolving processes
\cite{vinci2025onlinediscoverysimulationmodels}.

Equation~\ref{eq:mean_transfer_decomposition} separates the final mean deficit
without pretending that the two sources can be identified case by case. For
the final \textit{visit-only} cap-three baseline,
\begin{equation}
39.742-33.812=(37.293-33.812)+(39.742-37.293)
=3.481+2.450=5.930\ \text{minutes}.
\end{equation}
Approximately 59\,\% of the mean deficit is therefore already present relative
to the training-period mean. The remaining 41\,\% is the later-period mean
shift.
This decomposition applies to the means only and is not an additive
decomposition of EMD.

The service-time comparison shows a behavior in the same direction. An earlier
training-period diagnostic for the workload-blind rules bundle produced a
frequency-weighted yard-service EMD of 0.595 minutes, whereas the standardized
\textit{visit-only} reference reaches 2.341 minutes against the test period. The
comparison is descriptive, because the bundle versions are not identical, but
it is consistent with temporal transfer in activity timing.
Complete turnaround still has a substantial training-period deficit, which
indicates an additional model boundary beyond a drift. This distinction is
important to mention, as a direct future-log comparison alone can confuse the simulator
error with changing process behavior \cite{Ozdemir2025UtilityBPS}.

distributional distance.  A simulator can reproduce the basic inter-arrival
distribution while generating arrivals at the wrong times of day or with the
wrong peak behavior.  This is especially relevant in a logistics process
with strong calendar effects.  The reported inter-arrival EMD should therefore
be interpreted as a necessary but insufficient as a stand alone condition for arrival-process
fidelity.

\section{Transition Time Within Composite Pre-Service Delay}
\label{sec:results_percentile_sens}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

An earlier pipeline used route-specific transition-time baselines to
reconstruct enablement, whereas the final simulation uses ProSiT's
model-derived enablement mechanism (Section~\ref{sec:lifecycle_events}). The
estimates are therefore retained only as a scale check. Across the 48
gate-to-yard and yard-to-gate transitions with at least twenty observations,
fifth-percentile transition times range from 2.16 to 5.28 minutes. The
frequency-weighted estimates are 3.88 minutes inbound and 3.75 minutes
outbound, or 7.62 minutes per visit. This is comparable in scale to the
5.930-minute mean turnaround underprediction in
Table~\ref{tab:final_kpis}. The comparison indicates that unobserved transition
time may contribute materially to the residual, but it is neither an additive
correction nor a causal decomposition.

Separating movement from waiting is a recognised difficulty in data-driven
simulation for physical logistics. Reviews report that queue and resource
behaviour often cannot be recovered from conventional event logs because the
required operational state is missing
\cite{Sulis2026SimulationReview,Grobis2025ProductionLogistics}. A recent
production-logistics application decomposes an observed inter-activity interval
into transport and waiting only where buffer state identifies the two
components. Transport is then represented explicitly, while waiting emerges
from resource and buffer logic \cite{Grobis2026SimulationDevelopment}. CTB has
neither enablement, trajectory, nor queue-state observations for such a
decomposition. ProSiT models execution, model-derived waiting, arrivals, and
resource availability, but provides no separate timed spatial-transfer
primitive \cite{Vinci2026prosit}. Physical transition time is therefore not
identifiable or directly executable within the present model. Simply adding a
transition-time draw would also count part of the undifferentiated gap already
fitted as pre-service delay. A valid extension must first observe or estimate
movement, refit the residual waiting component, and introduce explicit movement
semantics. Until then, the output is interpreted as \emph{composite pre-service
delay}, not as measured queueing time.

\section{Held-Out Bottleneck Analysis}
\label{sec:results_bottlenecks}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

A bottleneck analysis identifies long individual activity compared to a
frequent activity at which a large amount of system time accumulates. The
held-out log is therefore decomposed at event level. The recorded handling operations service time
is completion minus start. The non-negative gap from the previous event's
completion to the current event's start is called \emph{composite pre-service
delay}. For the sequential truck process, the sum of both components exactly
reconstructs each case turnaround. The pre-service delay time span can contain
movement, traffic and transfer, dispatching, and queueing. It is not interpreted
as a direct queue measurement in this thesis.

Across the 17,892 held-out visits, mean turnaround is 39.742 minutes. Recorded
handling contributes 14.755 minutes per visit and composite pre-service delay
contributes 24.987 minutes. The decomposition error is zero for every case.
Table~\ref{tab:bottleneck_burden} ranks activities by their frequency-weighted
contribution rather than their mean duration alone.

The analysis distinguishes two model roles. The cap-three
\textit{visit-only} bundle remains the historical-fidelity reference and acts
as a workload-blind negative control. The cap-three \textit{native-only}

The expert-repaired sequential net also stays fixed. The experiments can change arrival

generate unseen sequential combinations of the known yard activities. Extra
activity types, detours represented by new events, and unmodelled exception
mechanisms remain outside the model. All scenario results are conditional on
this discovered control-flow language.

The operational question is not simply whether a parameter can be changed.
For terminal planning, the relevant question is how a forecast condition and a
candidate response propagate through process executions: for example, whether
reducing the number of truck slots released per hour under forecast high
utilisation protects turnaround, or whether a resource policy shifts delay from
receive to delivery handling. The scenario KPIs are therefore reported both at
case level and separately for the principal RMG handling patterns. This makes
the experiments decision-oriented while retaining the distinction between an
effect inside the simulation and an effect established for the physical

\subsection{Scenario A: T22 Resource-Pool Reallocation}

T22 is removed from every eligible RMG pool and its calendar is closed. The
intervention therefore executes as specified. In the
\textit{native-only} scenario model, the mean-turnaround change is
$-0.030\;[-0.168,+0.107]$ minutes and the P90 change is
$+0.100\;[-0.428,+0.628]$ minutes. Both paired intervals include zero. Mean
RMG service changes by $+0.006\;[-0.053,+0.066]$ minutes, whereas mean RMG
pre-service delay changes by $-0.093\;[-0.146,-0.040]$ minutes. Receive
service and pre-service changes are unresolved. Delivery service and
pre-service changes are also unresolved. The model therefore resolves a small
decrease in aggregate model-derived RMG pre-service delay, with no end-to-end
or service-time effect. The workload-blind \textit{visit-only} negative control
gives the same qualitative conclusion, with a mean-turnaround change of
$-0.003\;[-0.133,+0.128]$ minutes and a pre-service change of
$+0.065\;[+0.010,+0.119]$ minutes. Removing an eligible statistical resource
is observable, whereas a physical block-closure effect is not established.

The experiment is not a full block-closure study. The remaining 21 blocks stay
eligible for all RMG activities. Each keeps capacity three. The selector moves
T22 work to another statistical resource. It does not check container
location, block compatibility, relocation, or added travel. The result only
tests the abstract resource pool.

\subsection{Scenario B: 20\,\% Higher Demand}

The working-time intervention is exact. Mean inter-arrival time changes from
0.6830 to 0.5692 minutes. The implied rate rises from 87.842 to 105.411 arrivals
per working hour, or 20\,\%. The rate measured over elapsed calendar time rises
by 15.910\,\%. Closed gate periods remain fixed. Timestamps are also floored to
minutes. This flooring turns more short positive intervals into simultaneous
observations. It does not change the zero mass in the input distribution.

Mean turnaround changes by $-0.087\;[-0.281,+0.106]$ minutes, mean RMG service
by $+0.049\;[-0.041,+0.140]$ minutes, and mean RMG pre-service by
$-0.062\;[-0.139,+0.014]$ minutes. The receive and delivery mean service and
pre-service intervals also include zero. The \textit{native-only} model
therefore transmits the intervention to its dynamic state but does not produce
a resolved mean handling or end-to-end response at this demand level.

The result is interpreted together with the response surface below. A 20\,\%
change in working-time intensity produces only a 15.910\,\% increase in
elapsed arrival rate and remains below the fitted model's transition region.
It cannot show that the real terminal could absorb 20\,\% more demand without
congestion. The model still lacks container location, physical movement,
ship-side crane allocation, and an observed queue against which its internal
queue state could be validated.

\subsection{Objective Assignment of Model Roles}

The four-cell validation experiment and the paired stress test answer
different questions. Under the pre-specified RQ3 rule, \textit{visit-only} is
the historical-fidelity reference because no added state source produces a
resolved validation improvement. Relative to that reference,
\textit{native-only} changes turnaround EMD by
$+0.105\;[-0.099,+0.308]$ minutes, weighted yard-service EMD by
$+0.037\;[-0.037,+0.112]$ minutes, activity-rate L1 by
$+0.0006\;[-0.0032,+0.0044]$, and 3-gram NGD by
$+0.00006\;[-0.00032,+0.00044]$. There is no statistically resolved
deterioration, although this is not a formal non-inferiority conclusion.

Historical equivalence within these intervals is not enough for RQ4. The
workload-blind model fails the intervention-response requirement, while the
native-state model produces a clear high-load response. \textit{Native-only}
is therefore assigned the scenario-model role. It is preferred over
\textit{both} because the latter adds static historical proxies that do not
update with the simulated arrival intervention. It is preferred over
\textit{static-only} because static proxies cannot provide an endogenous
queue state. This decision is purpose-specific. It does not overturn the RQ3
result or claim that \textit{native-only} is globally the best model.

The demand-response criterion was added after the workload-blind stress
diagnostic. It is consequently an adaptive analysis and not part of the
pre-specified historical selection. Reporting both stages preserves the
evidential distinction. RQ1 evaluates historical reproduction with the
selected \textit{visit-only} model. RQ4 evaluates intervention behavior with
the minimal responsive \textit{native-only} model and uses
\textit{visit-only} as a negative control.

\medskip

\noindent\textbf{RQ4 answer.} Both policy interventions execute as specified.
T22 assignments fall to zero, and working-time arrival intensity rises by
exactly 20\,\%. The workload-blind T22 negative control produces no resolved
end-to-end change and only a small increase in model-derived RMG pre-service
delay. In the \textit{native-only} demand model, the 20\,\% intervention also
produces no resolved handling or mean-turnaround response. The capacity audit
shows why this is plausible inside the fitted model. The most-loaded held-out
block reaches only 0.506 nominal mean utilisation under the three-task
ceiling.

The paired stress test closes the more important functional question. The
\textit{visit-only} model remains invariant even at the highest tested demand,
whereas \textit{native-only} develops a resolved delay response between
realised arrival-rate ratios 2.367 and 2.825. At ratio 3.504, mean turnaround
increases by 4.069 minutes and mean RMG pre-service by 5.221 minutes. The
matched native-minus-visit contrasts confirm that this difference is not
explained by Monte-Carlo variation. The retained T13 queue-length split gives
an inspectable statistical mechanism for the non-linear response.

This capability is the operational reason for simulation. Candidate truck-slot
rates and handling policies can be compared before they are applied. The
reported transition region is nevertheless a property of the fitted
simulation, not a physical CTB congestion threshold. Spatial assignment,
movement, equipment allocation, and observed queue semantics remain
insufficient for a prescriptive recommendation. Executability and a plausible
stress response do not establish counterfactual validity.

\section{Interpretation: From Process Mining to Data-Aware Simulation}
\label{sec:results_interpretation}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The results become most informative when the structural, temporal and
scenario findings are interpreted together. They do not describe one model
that is simply ``good'' or ``bad.'' Instead, they show that different forms of
process-mining evidence answer different questions. The compact repaired
source net reproduces the test control flow while generalising across sequential
yard combinations, the arrival model reproduces an important part of the
workload timing, and the simulator remains too fast at the level of complete
truck visits. These findings are compatible because control-flow conformance,
arrival fidelity, and end-to-end temporal fidelity are distinct properties
\cite{Camargo2021,ChapelaCampa2023Trust,Chapela2025SimQuality}.

\paragraph{Meaning for traditional Process Mining}
Traditional Process Mining is strongest in this study as a language for
reconstructing and checking observed process behavior. Here, its most
important diagnostic is not merely complete test fitness. Preserving the ITS
schedule-derived stop order removes a timestamp-sorting artefact, but the raw
64\,\% Inductive-Miner tree still contains three parallel operators. The
algorithm interprets reversed sequential relations as concurrency even though
terminal experts state that one truck is serviced sequentially. The final tree
has no parallel operator because this semantic conflict is repaired explicitly,
not because discovery alone resolves it. This demonstrates a classical
Process-Mining point with direct simulation consequences: the event-log
ordering relation and the domain meaning of a discovered operator both require
validation
\cite{PMRozinat2009,PMvanDerAalst2016}.

The final model makes the fitness--precision--generalisation trade-off visible.
It fits 100\,\% of test traces and retains a test generalisation of
0.9488, including behavior not stored as an exact development path. Precision is
lower at 0.7641 because the loop admits additional yard combinations and
repetitions. In this case, that broader language is not equivalent to physical
parallelism and is not automatically an error: the terminal process allows
varying sequential activity orders. The domain check must therefore be
semantic and operator-specific. Penalising every unseen order would collapse
discovery into memorisation, while accepting a parallel cut would violate the
truck-visit contract.

The silent gate-only path in the raw tree makes the same trade-off concrete.
It is retained in the discovery audit but removed from the executable source
model because experts reject a visit without yard work. Formal language
analysis and realised-output validation still answer complementary questions:
the former identifies what could happen, while the latter confirms that all
final replications satisfy the repaired contract.

This is a precision-side model-repair problem. The simulation-guided
control-flow repair framework \cite{SimulationGuidedRepair2025} provides a rigorous way to
identify differences between real and simulated control flow and to compare an
original and repaired net on held-out data. Its published operators currently
add missing behavior, however, whereas the CTB bypass requires restricting
excess behavior. A state-aware stochastic Petri-net study
\cite{Vinci2023Inf.Activ.Prob} demonstrates that an explicit manual restriction before simulation can be defensible when a
source model permits a minimal trace absent from the log. For CTB, the
restriction is therefore documented as the discovery-to-execution repair and
applied before every ProSiT configuration is fitted. It is neither hidden nor
selected from validation performance.

The same separation applies to time. Good control-flow coverage does not
account for the 5.930-minute mean turnaround deficit or the 13.100-minute P90
deficit. The simulator can put activities in the correct order and still omit
time between them. In the CTB setting, this missing time may contain movement,
traffic, resource interaction, queueing or operational states that are not
represented as events. The temporal bias is therefore not a contradiction of
the conformance result. It identifies a different abstraction boundary. This
is why evaluating an event-log-derived simulator only through replay fitness
or variant frequencies would have produced an overly positive conclusion.

The transition-baseline diagnostic makes the scale of that abstraction
boundary concrete. Route-specific p5 values lie between 2.16 and 5.28 minutes,
and the frequency-weighted inbound and outbound lower-tail components sum to
7.62 minutes per visit. Their proximity to the 5.930-minute mean deficit is
consistent with omitted physical movement being a major contributor. It does
not prove that all residual time is travel, because the baseline also contains
unseparated traffic and operational delay and because a percentile summary
cannot be subtracted case by case. The fair conclusion is therefore stronger
than merely saying that ``some time is missing,'' but weaker than claiming a
validated travel-time mechanism.

\paragraph{Meaning for data-aware Process Mining}
Recent data-aware methods extend conventional discovery by conditioning
routing, duration or resource behavior on case attributes and process state
\cite{mannhardt2023stochasticProcessesModelling,Vinci2023Inf.Activ.Prob,Vinci2026prosit}.
The CTB results show both the value and the limit of that extension. Its value
is selective rather than universal. Calendar context contains a strong and
stable signal for arrivals: the time-aware benchmark reduces arrival-count
MAE by 67.5\,\% and RMSE by 51.2\,\%, and the day arrival rate is about 8.7
times the night rate. In this part of the process, conditioning on information
available at case creation captures a real predictive regularity.

The duration results point in the other direction. For the two dominant RMG
activities, the diagnostic decision trees have slightly negative test
$R^2$. In the factorial validation experiment, none of the three
additional-state configurations achieves a resolved improvement over
\textit{visit-only} without a resolved loss elsewhere. Static demand,
utilisation and rank variables do appear in learned rules, and the native
models retain one queue-length split. Their interpretability therefore does
not imply improved temporal transfer. This is not proof that workload never
matters. It shows that the available CTB representations do not add validated
predictive value under the tested specification.

This qualification is central to the interpretation of data awareness.
Adding attributes increases representational capacity, but useful capacity
depends on information timing, measurement quality and a relationship that is
stable across the training and application regimes. Case-level target demand
or utilisation may be correlated with an observed duration without measuring
the physical mechanism that caused it. Conversely, a weak marginal
relationship can hide omitted event-level states such as the actual queue,
truck position or equipment movement. The white-box character of ProSiT is
valuable here because the learned conditions can be inspected and challenged.
interpretability makes the association auditable, but does not convert it into
causality or guarantee predictive validity
\cite{Vinci2026prosit,Sargent2013Validation}.

This result sharpens rather than contradicts earlier process-state-abstraction
experiments \cite{Vinci2023Inf.Activ.Prob}. The best-performing abstraction
varies between the two evaluated processes, and an unsuitable history state can
create problematic simulated behavior. The CTB ablation supplies the missing
application-specific test: neither workload source improves the joint
validation criteria sufficiently to replace the parsimonious visit/process
state. It also goes beyond data-aware routing alone. The framework of
López-Pintado et al. \cite{lopez2024} demonstrates the value of case data for
control-flow decisions but identifies data-dependent activity duration as a
separate extension. CTB shows why that separation matters in physical
logistics: state-dependent firing probabilities cannot by themselves create
travel, queues, or spatially constrained service.

The resource result is equally consistent with the differentiated-resource
evidence of López-Pintado and Dumas \cite{BPSdifferentiatedResources2022}.
Treating resources as statistically interchangeable can preserve event counts
while distorting timing. In CTB, capping nominal RMG concurrency does not make
target assignment spatially sticky and does not model crane or truck movement.
By contrast, physical container-terminal simulation represents travel,
equipment interaction and allocation policies explicitly
\cite{Kastner2021}. The current hybrid research opportunity is therefore to
retain the discovered white-box process and state rules while supplying a
mechanistic movement, queue, and resource-assignment layer.

\paragraph{Correlation, scenarios, and operational meaning}
The results therefore distinguish predictive association from intervention
response. The temporal arrival features show a strong association with when
trucks enter the terminal, but the 20\,\% arrival intervention produces no
resolved mean-turnaround increase. This combination does not imply that real
CTB turnaround is independent of demand. It shows that the fitted simulator
contains an arrival relationship, while the offered-load calculation places
the chosen change below mean block saturation. The paired ladder adds the
missing functional test. The workload-blind model does not respond even under
extreme demand, while the minimal native-state model develops a strong
pre-service and turnaround response once the realised rate passes the
independently estimated capacity region. The only retained dynamic split in
the final waiting model links T13 queue length to two different waiting-time
distributions. This provides a direct explanation of the response inside the
simulator without establishing the same threshold as a physical law. In the
same way, eliminating T22
assignments verifies that the resource rule was
changed, while the near-zero turnaround effect reflects reallocation within an
abstract interchangeable resource pool. It is not evidence that closing a
physical block would have no operational effect.

Taken together, the scenario results act as stress tests of the model's
semantics. A null effect can be a substantive operational finding only when
the mechanisms through which the intervention should propagate are represented
and validated. Here, the saturation experiment demonstrates a capacity-driven
response only when native dynamic state is enabled. This separates historical
replay fidelity from intervention validity, but it does not validate the
physical route through which that response would arise. Location feasibility,
movement, differentiated crane availability and spatial queue state remain
absent. The contribution of the
data-aware white-box model is consequently twofold: it supports reproducible
and inspectable changes, and it makes the boundary between an implemented
association and a credible physical mechanism visible. That boundary is the
main result for decision support. The model is suitable for analysing
conditional consequences inside its documented abstraction, but not yet for
claiming causal terminal effects from the scenarios or stress ladder.

The target application nevertheless remains concrete. Before publishing truck
appointments, a terminal could combine a utilisation forecast with candidate
hourly slot limits, simulate each policy, and compare turnaround together with
receive- and delivery-specific service and pre-service KPIs. If forecast
conditions threaten retrieval performance, a candidate policy could reduce
the admitted truck rate or prioritise retrieval-compatible resources. The
simulator would quantify both the intended benefit and any transferred delay
to delivery operations. This prospective comparison is the benefit that a
validated logistics simulation should provide. The CTB experiments establish
the transparent experimental interface and show exactly which additional
queue, location, and equipment states must be represented before that interface
can be used prescriptively.

The boundary is systematic. Traditional Process Mining supports the first two
layers particularly well: it reveals ordering semantics, generalisation, and
excess language. Data-aware simulation adds auditable conditional routing and
timing rules and supports the third layer where suitable state is available.
The later layers require mechanisms that are not implied by correlation alone.
Recent work is moving in the same direction: evolving-process discovery
addresses drift \cite{vinci2025onlinediscoverysimulationmodels}, and temporal
deviation analysis seeks to attribute simulation mismatch to arrival,
processing, and resource inputs rather than reporting magnitude alone
\cite{Wang2026TemporalDeviations}. The CTB study contributes a physical-
logistics instance in which those layers fail at different points.

This framing also clarifies the role of expert intervention. A hospital
simulation-discovery application combines automatic discovery with manual
structural changes and expert plausibility assessment before optimisation
\cite{Hospital2026Vinci}. The CTB source-model repair follows the same
principle of explicit and auditable domain knowledge. A comparable
expert validation of queue, travel and resource semantics remains necessary
before slot-rate or handling-priority recommendations are presented as terminal
counterfactuals.

\section{Methodological Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

\paragraph{Pre-Service Delay}

ProSiT enables an activity when the process model allows it. Consecutive CTB
activities can occur at different locations, but truck movement is not a
separate activity in the event-log design. The time from completion of one
activity to the start of the next can therefore combine
\begin{equation}
    D_{\mathrm{pre\text{-}service}} \approx
    T_{\mathrm{physical}} +
    T_{\mathrm{traffic}} +
    T_{\mathrm{queue}} +
    T_{\mathrm{other}}.
\end{equation}
The log cannot identify these parts separately. The percentile check shows
that a reconstructed baseline is sensitive to its assumption. It does not
measure any one part. This would require position, trajectory, queue-entry, or
similar state data. These data are not available. The results use
the term \textit{pre-service delay}, not queueing time.

\paragraph{Service-Time Semantics and Downstream Implications}

The interval from \texttt{BEREITMELDUNG} (reported ready) to
\texttt{ERFUELLUNG} (fulfilled) is labelled \emph{service time} for
consistency with the BPS literature, but it is more accurately a
ready-to-fulfilment stage duration.  It may include resource-access delay,
dispatching latency, or short operational waits that occur between the
reported-ready instant and the start of active handling.  This distinction
matters because the same variable enters duration-distribution fitting,
service-time EMD comparisons, resource-capacity assumptions, and the
offered-load calculations used for the demand-saturation diagnostic.  If
congestion-related waiting is partially included in what is called service
time, then the offered-load estimate may overstate effective utilisation and
the capacity-saturation region may be shifted.  The thesis retains the term to
avoid divergence from the framework it builds on, but the downstream
interpretations should be read with this qualification.

\paragraph{Resource Assignment Is Not Spatial Container Relocation}

An RMG resource in ProSiT is an abstract capacity assignment. The discovered
overlap capacity is 100. The stated physical cap is 66 across 22 blocks. This
mismatch shows that timestamp overlap is not a crane count. The selected
scenario reference model uses the cap of three per block.

The cap does not add spatial state. Target-area attributes are predictors, not
hard inventory constraints. The simulator does not bind a container to a
storage block. It also does not create relocation work after reassignment.
Closing T22 spreads its statistical demand across the remaining blocks. It
does not model feasibility, crane movement, or added truck travel. Scenario~A
tests the resource-pool abstraction. It does not forecast a physical T22
closure.

\paragraph{Initial State at the Temporal Cutoff}

Each replication starts from the Petri-net initial marking with no active
truck cases and empty resource schedules. Trucks that entered before the test
cutoff but remained inside the real terminal are not reconstructed. The
historical comparison is therefore conditional on an empty simulated system
at the beginning of the period. This can bias the earliest generated cases and
is one reason not to interpret the test as a live digital-twin initialization.
All matched scenario alternatives share exactly the same empty state, so this
boundary does not by itself create their paired differences. Operational use
would require work-in-progress initialization or a documented warm-up design.

\paragraph{Scope of Expert Validation}

The project-level expert review confirmed the gate boundaries, the requirement
for at least one yard activity, arbitrary yard ordering and repetition, the
validity of \texttt{LL\_mixed}-only yard visits and the invalidity of gate-only
visits. It also confirmed two hard physical constraints. One truck does not
execute activities in parallel, and no block can use more than three RMGs
concurrently. These judgments support the case contract, operator check,
bypass diagnosis and cap-three correction. They do not validate the discovered
HO2 or LL capacities, the fully open resource calendars, arrival and duration
distributions, block-interchangeability assumption, pre-service-delay
semantics, or any scenario response. The review was a requirements check and
did not produce a formal transcript or inter-rater assessment. Its evidential
scope is therefore narrower than a structured expert-validation study.

\paragraph{The Empirical Population Excludes Multi-RMG Visits}

The case-level ProSiT representation requires one unambiguous RMG target for
the block-specific context variables. The pipeline therefore removes 1,693
visits and 6,820 events involving more than one RMG block. The results apply to
the resulting one-RMG subprocess, not to the complete population of CTB truck
movements. The exclusion removes some of the visits most likely to contain
additional movement, cross-block resource interaction and spatial complexity.
It may therefore make transport and congestion behavior easier than in the
full terminal population. An object-centric representation could preserve the
truck, containers and multiple blocks as related objects instead of forcing one
block value onto the complete visit
\cite{VanDerAalst2023OCPM,Knopp2023ObjectCentricSimulation}. This is a direct
empirical scope boundary, not only a general future-work opportunity.

\paragraph{Scenario Control Flow Generalises Only Within a Fixed Activity Set}

The Inductive-Miner loop can generate sequential combinations and repetitions
that were not present as complete training variants. This is less restrictive
than the earlier trie representation, but it is not an open-ended terminal
model. A scenario cannot create a new activity type, an unrecorded exception
mechanism, or a structurally different response to disruption. The two policy
experiments and the demand ladder therefore test parameter changes inside the
discovered activity language. Missing structural mechanisms may still remove
real sources of delay.

\paragraph{Systematic Turnaround Under-Prediction}

The data-aware reference model under-predicts mean turnaround by 5.930 minutes and P90 by
13.100 minutes. The narrow seed intervals show that this is model bias, not
simulation noise. The log cannot show how much missing time comes from
movement, traffic, queueing, or an unrecorded state. Correct case order does
not remove this limit on KPI forecasts.

\paragraph{Scenario Intervals Cover Only Part of Structural Uncertainty}

The matched-seed intervals in Tables~\ref{tab:scenario_kpis},
\ref{tab:demand_scenario_kpis}, and
\ref{tab:demand_saturation_ladder} cover random
variation in one fixed model. They do not cover a new training period, another
discovery run, or missing spatial state. The cap sets a ceiling but adds no
location feasibility or equipment movement. More seeds would narrow these
intervals. They would not reduce structural uncertainty. The matched
state-configuration comparison addresses one part of that uncertainty: it
shows that high-load response depends on whether native dynamic state is
available. It still does not cover a new training period, another discovered net,
alternative resource semantics, or the missing spatial and queueing state.

\paragraph{External Validity Is Limited to One Terminal and Time Window}

The temporal hold-out is one later period from the same terminal and source
systems. Near-perfect replay fitness shows structural stability in this
period, but the observed 2.450-minute mean-turnaround shift shows that temporal
stationarity does not hold even within the study window. The results do not
cover another terminal, season, gate regime, or infrastructure setup. That
would require another full discovery and validation run. An adaptive or
rolling-window simulator would additionally require prospective evaluation.

\paragraph{Marginal Arrival Validation Does Not Establish Process-Level
Fidelity}

The inter-arrival EMD of 0.072 minutes compares marginal gap distributions.
It does not assess hour-of-day arrival profiles, weekday effects, burst-size
distributions, temporal autocorrelation, or conditional dependencies between
arrival time and visit characteristics.  A simulator can reproduce the
marginal inter-arrival distribution while misplacing arrivals within the day
or suppressing realistic arrival clustering.  These dimensions are not
validated in the current study and should be assessed before the arrival model
is used for time-of-day-sensitive policy comparisons.

\paragraph{Event-Linkage Reliability}

The cross-system identifier match uses plate number and minute-resolved
timestamp.  This composite key works on the evaluated data, but it remains
vulnerable to duplicate plates at the same minute, delayed or retroactive
event recording, and rounding artefacts in other extraction periods.  The
findings therefore demonstrate validity for the evaluated data-generation
process, not unconditional reliability of the linkage procedure across
arbitrary terminal periods or source-system versions.
