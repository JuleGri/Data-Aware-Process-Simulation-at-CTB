\chapter{Conclusion}
\label{chp:conclusion}

The research objective was to evaluate the fidelity, structural applicability,
and limitations of automatically discovered data-aware process simulation for
container-terminal truck operations. The CTB case study shows that these
aspects must be assessed together. The simulator is reproducible, transparent,
and structurally stable. However, historical fidelity and executable scenarios
do not prove that the model can predict physical effects.

\section{Answer to the Research Objective}

The approach can be applied to CTB truck operations as a transparent model,
but its use for decision support is limited. The final model reproduces the
yard-service and arrival distributions with some remaining error and keeps the
physical order of a truck visit. However, it under-predicts turnaround time,
and more contextual features do not improve held-out fidelity in the available
data. The what-if experiments can be reproduced, but their weak congestion
response shows that spatial container state, equipment movement, and queue
mechanisms are missing. The model can be used to study its process abstraction
and identify modelling limits. It is not yet enough for direct recommendations
about the physical terminal.

\section{Answers to the Research Questions}

\begin{description}
\item[RQ1 (Fidelity).] The final calibrated simulator is structurally stable
      across ten seeds and reproduces yard-service and arrival distributions,
      but it under-predicts mean turnaround by 9.306 minutes and P90 by 19.200
      minutes. The model reproduces parts of the observed behaviour but not
      the full turnaround-time distribution.
\item[RQ2 (Structural applicability).] The sequential observed-variant trie
      replays 99.944\,\% of held-out traces without deviations and satisfies the
      required Gate In--yard--Gate Out sequence. Its strict case language is a
      suitable simulation contract, with reduced behavioural generality as
      an explicit trade-off.
\item[RQ3 (Contextual expressiveness).] Increasing contextual expressiveness
      from \textit{no-rules} to \textit{rules$+$workload} does not improve
      held-out fidelity. The more expressive endpoint has higher turnaround
      distance and higher cross-seed variation. More context only helps when
      the available attributes explain the relevant operational variation.
\item[RQ4 (What-if decision support).] The T22 and demand interventions are
      transparent and reproducible, but they do not yield a resolved
      mean-turnaround response. Their behaviour reveals that scenario
      executability is not equivalent to counterfactual validity when
      physical locations, relocation feasibility, equipment movements, and
      explicit queues are absent from the abstraction.
\end{description}

\section{Personal Takeaways}
The project led to the following personal takeaways:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Independent Project Management:} I learned to organise my
    work, manage deadlines, and complete a large project independently.

    \item \textbf{Learning from Setbacks:} Failed attempts, unexpected results,
    and debugging were part of the research process and helped improve the work.

    \item \textbf{First Principles Thinking:} When a problem became difficult, it helped to return to the basic mathematics and logic before continuing.

    \item \textbf{Reproducibility Over Complexity:} A tested and documented
    framework is more useful than a system that cannot be managed. Small and
    tested scripts made the codebase easier to handle.

    \item \textbf{Working with Others:} Discussions and feedback from the research group improved the final work.
\end{enumerate}
