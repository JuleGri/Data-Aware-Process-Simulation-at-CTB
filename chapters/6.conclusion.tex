\chapter{Conclusion}
\label{chp:conclusion}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

This thesis investigated how far an automatically discovered, data-aware
process simulation can be transferred to a physically constrained logistics
environment. Two months of operational truck-handling data from the HHLA
Container Terminal Burchardkai were transformed into a simulation-ready event
log and used to discover an executable ProSiT model. The evaluation did not
equate successful execution with validity. It examined the discovered control
flow, historical and temporal fidelity, the effect of different state
representations, physical constraints, and the response to operational
interventions as separate claims.

The strongest result concerns control flow. Preserving the ITS
schedule-derived stop order stored in XES allows Inductive Miner to discover a
compact Petri net without parallel behaviour inside a truck visit. Only 11 of
89,460 visits change sequence under completion-time sorting, yet these rare
inversions are sufficient to produce an incorrect parallel cut when timestamps
are treated as the ordering relation. On the temporal hold-out, the final net
reaches a fitness of 0.999992 and completely fits 99.994\,\% of 17,892 visits.
Its precision of 0.7558 reflects additional sequential yard combinations and a
silent gate-only bypass, not physically impossible concurrency of one truck.
The broader internal ordering and repetition of yard activities are consistent
with the expert-validated terminal process. The gate-only bypass is not. A
separate precision-oriented repair removes this path and raises precision to
0.7939 while reducing generalisation from 0.9413 to 0.8859. The unchanged and
repaired bundles produce identical EMD, activity-rate, case-contract, and
3-gram NGD results in a matched ten-seed comparison. The repair therefore improves
the formal language without changing realised baseline behaviour. The
unchanged net is retained as the auditable automatic-discovery baseline, while
the repair documents the trade-off introduced by a domain restriction. This
supports a positive answer to RQ2 at the structural level, but not an
unqualified claim of physical validity.

The unusual \texttt{LL\_mixed} branch has a narrow empirical basis. In 57 of
64 observed visits containing \texttt{LL\_mixed}, it is the only yard activity.
Of the seven combined visits, six training cases place it first before
\texttt{RMG\_receive}, while one held-out case places it after
\texttt{RMG\_delivery}. An empty-container exchange performed first for routing
reasons is therefore a plausible interpretation, but not a validated universal
rule. Its rarity also means that \texttt{LL\_mixed} contributes only
0.0328\,\% of held-out turnaround burden and does not constitute a
frequency-weighted bottleneck.

The answer to RQ1 is mixed. The selected cap-three \textit{visit-only} model
reproduces arrivals closely, with an inter-arrival EMD of 0.072 minutes. Its
yard-service EMD is 3.150 minutes without frequency weighting and 2.341 minutes
when weighted by the real activity frequencies. Complete truck turnaround is
less accurate. Mean turnaround is 33.812 minutes in simulation and 39.742
minutes in the hold-out, a deficit of 5.930 minutes or 14.92\,\%. Simulated P90
is 57.900 rather than 71.000 minutes. The narrow intervals across ten
Monte-Carlo seeds show that this is systematic model error rather than
simulation noise.

Two analyses delimit that error without claiming a causal decomposition. Real
mean turnaround increases by 2.450 minutes between the training and hold-out
periods, so the temporal evaluation combines model mismatch with operational
change. Route-specific fifth-percentile transition baselines range from 2.16
to 5.28 minutes. Their frequency-weighted boundary contribution is 7.62
minutes per visit, comparable in scale to the mean turnaround deficit. These values
support a non-zero movement and transfer component between recorded
activities, but they do not separate travel, traffic, dispatching, access
delay, and queueing. Adding them to the current pre-service distributions
would risk counting the same elapsed time twice.

The held-out bottleneck decomposition reinforces this interpretation. Of the
39.742-minute real mean turnaround, recorded handling contributes 14.755
minutes and composite pre-service delay contributes 24.987 minutes. The gap
before \texttt{Gate Out} is the largest single accumulated component. RMG
receive is the largest handling-related accumulation point and accounts for
27.99\,\% of reconstructed held-out time when service and pre-service delay are
combined. These are time-burden findings, not proof of a physical queue or a
capacity shortage, because the event log does not observe the mechanisms
inside the gaps.

RQ3 shows that additional state does not automatically improve simulation
quality. The standardized $2\times2$ experiment separates static
demand/utilisation proxies from ProSiT's native workload and queue state.
Static proxies increase the yard-activity-rate error by about 0.063 and
3-gram NGD by about 0.0165, irrespective of whether native state is present.
Native state leaves these control-flow measures almost unchanged, but raises
turnaround EMD by 0.097 minutes without static proxies and by 0.310 minutes
with them. Their turnaround interaction is 0.213 minutes. No weighted
yard-service improvement is resolved. A semantically cleaned static candidate
set retains no demand or utilisation split and still worsens activity-rate and
local-sequence fidelity. This is evidence of feature-candidate-set sensitivity,
not repeated-sampling evidence of statistical discovery instability. The NGD
ranking is unchanged when the ITS order stored in XES replaces completion-time
ordering in the sensitivity calculation.

The parsimonious \textit{visit-only} bundle is consequently used as the
within-study reference. This choice is deliberately qualified. The same
temporal hold-out was used to compare the four configurations and to report the
selected model's fidelity. It is therefore not an independently selected
prospective model. The experiment nevertheless establishes an important
substantive result. The available contextual attributes and native ProSiT state
do not supply the missing physical state. Data awareness can condition model
behaviour on observed information, but it cannot reconstruct truck position,
container location, equipment movement, spatial queues, or changing
waterside--landside allocation when those relations are absent from the event
log.

RQ4 makes this validity boundary visible. Both operational interventions are
implemented reproducibly. Removing T22 represents a plausible construction,
system, infrastructure, or mechanical-failure condition and eliminates all
T22 assignments. It changes mean turnaround by $+0.110$ minutes with a 95\,\%
interval of $[-0.057,+0.277]$, while model-derived RMG pre-service delay rises
by a resolved 0.118 minutes. Increasing working-time arrival intensity by
20\,\% represents a plausible change in admitted truck volume through slot and
gate-lane decisions. It changes mean turnaround by $-0.033$ minutes with an
interval of $[-0.171,+0.104]$ and produces no resolved mean handling response.
Repeating both interventions with the rejected \textit{both} bundle preserves
the mean-turnaround conclusion, although selected tail and component responses
remain bundle-sensitive.

These scenarios demonstrate transparent parameter intervention, not the full
white-box intervention potential of ProSiT. T22 removal changes resource
mapping and availability. The demand experiment changes the empirical samples
of one arrival leaf. Neither experiment changes a tree condition, threshold,
or subtree. A retained T13 waiting-time rule illustrates that such structure is
inspectable, but it is not interpreted as a causal queueing law. The 20\,\%
intervention also remains below nominal mean saturation in the independent
offered-load diagnostic. A separate historical demand ladder shows that an
earlier fully contextualised bundle propagates sufficiently strong abstract
capacity pressure into additional delay. Because it does not use the final
\textit{visit-only} bundle, it neither explains that model's weak response nor
establishes its saturation threshold.

The expert-validated ceiling of three RMGs per block illustrates the
difference between empirical parameter discovery and physical admissibility.
Overlap-derived capacities above three are retained as discovery evidence but
are capped in the effective scenario model. The earlier capped-versus-uncapped
diagnostic does not resolve a direct mean-turnaround effect or a cap-by-scenario
interaction. The ceiling is therefore retained because the uncapped state is
physically impossible, not because it produces the headline findings.

The scientific contribution is the resulting layered audit of automatic
simulation-model generation in port logistics. The thesis provides a
reproducible CTB event-log and ProSiT workflow, a temporal and multi-seed
evaluation, an empirical comparison of alternative state sources, and a clear
separation between discovered parameters, expert-supplied constraints, and
scenario interventions. More importantly, it demonstrates why control-flow
conformance, stochastic fidelity, temporal transfer, physical semantics, and
counterfactual validity must not be collapsed into one claim of simulation
accuracy. A model can have almost perfect held-out replay fitness while
underestimating turnaround. A richer feature set can perform worse than a
simpler one. A physically impossible capacity can be inferred from overlapping
timestamps. A technically correct intervention can still lack the mechanism
required for a physical forecast.

The empirical scope is the retained one-RMG subprocess rather than all CTB
truck movements. The case-level representation excludes 1,693 multi-RMG visits
and 6,820 events because it cannot attach multiple changing block states to one
truck case. The study may therefore underrepresent movement, cross-block
interaction, and spatial complexity. Within that boundary, the model is a
transparent historical and diagnostic simulation. It is not yet a
decision-valid digital twin. Its present operational value is to make model
assumptions, scenario mechanics, and evidence gaps inspectable. A future model
with validated location, movement, equipment, and queue state could support the
decision loop that motivates terminal simulation by comparing truck-slot,
resource, and handling policies before their implementation.

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:future_work}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

Future work should retain the reproducible discovery-and-validation protocol
while extending the mechanisms and evidence that currently limit physical and
counterfactual interpretation:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Separate physical movement from residual pre-service delay.}
    Route, position, or equipment observations are required to estimate
    transition-specific movement and then refit the remaining access and
    waiting component. This must be done as one decomposition so that physical
    transit is not added on top of a distribution that already contains it.

    \item \textbf{Represent spatial assignment, equipment state, and queues.}
    Containers and truck operations should be bound to physically feasible
    locations, with explicit resource movement and queue semantics. A coupled
    discrete-event, agent-based, or object-centric design could retain the
    discovered process model while representing mechanisms that conventional
    case-centric event data omit
    \cite{VanDerAalst2023OCPM,Knopp2023ObjectCentricSimulation}.

    \item \textbf{Evaluate temporal transfer prospectively.}
    Rolling or recency-weighted discovery should be tested across several later
    periods. Configuration selection and final validation should use separate
    temporal windows so that the selected model is evaluated independently.

    \item \textbf{Test an explicit white-box policy.}
    A context-dependent and expert-validated decision, such as a threshold for
    truck-slot release or receive/delivery priority, should be represented by a
    visible ProSiT rule, leaf, or subtree intervention. Its simulated effect
    should then be compared with historical natural experiments or prospective
    terminal observations before it is used for operational recommendations.
\end{itemize}
