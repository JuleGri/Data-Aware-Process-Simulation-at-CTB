Container terminals use simulation to assess operational changes before they
are made. Data-aware process simulation promises to learn such models from event
logs. It has rarely been applied in logistics, and the modelling of queues and
shared resources is the task that process mining supports least. This thesis
examines whether a simulation learned with ProSiT from the operational data of
HHLA Container Terminal Burchardkai can support the assessment of a load-aware
allocation of truck deliveries to yard blocks. The terminal aims at an hourly mean
turnaround time between 25 and 35 minutes. In the test period, the mean exceeds
35 minutes in 56\,\% of the hours.

From an event log of 91,153 truck visits, nineteen nets of four discovery
families were compared with checks fixed on the training log, namely soundness,
acceptance of every sequence above a coverage threshold of 0.1\,\%, and the
domain rules of the truck visit. The Heuristics Miner net HM 0.7, used as
discovered, is the only net that passes all checks (precision 0.998). A
look-ahead stop guard makes every simulated truck perform its planned number of
stops. As fitted, the simulation overestimates the turnaround time strongly
(median 57 against 33 minutes), because its learned block choice concentrates
the trucks. A component-wise audit traces this to how ProSiT counts queues and
workload and to a single-server view of the blocks. With replayed trucks and
blocks, three lanes per block, a waiting time measured from a free lane and
load-dependent service, the model reproduces the median within 5 minutes and the
rise of the waiting with the load of a block. It does not reproduce the slowdown
in the busiest hours, in either period. Its validity for the intended use is
therefore partial.

The allocation rule was evaluated within these limits, in six scenarios that
combine one, two or any block of deviation with the observed demand and 20\,\%
more trucks. In the model, moving deliveries at the gate away from blocks that
three trucks are already heading to lowers the share of hours whose mean
turnaround time exceeds 35 minutes by 8 to 13 percentage points in every
scenario, through less waiting at full blocks. A local rule gives most of the
effect, and under high demand a wider choice of blocks moves part of the waiting
to other trucks. The share of hours below 25 minutes shows no clear change. The terminal confirmed the
rules and the decision point and found the result plausible, but not the missing
peak hours. Because the model misses the congestion that the rule is meant to
relieve, the result is a hypothesis for the terminal, not an estimate of its
effect. The case shows that a satisfactory control flow can coexist with
inadequate congestion behaviour, and that the mechanism an intervention acts on
has to be validated in the simulator itself before a what-if result can be
trusted.
