Container terminals use simulation to evaluate operational changes without
disrupting live operations, but conventional models are commonly constructed
and calibrated by hand. Data-aware process simulation instead derives
executable models from event logs. Its validity in physical logistics remains
under-evaluated.

This thesis examines ProSiT for truck operations at HHLA Container Terminal
Burchardkai. Two months of operational data were transformed into an event log
of 89,460 truck visits. The log was divided chronologically into 64\,\%
discovery, 16\,\% validation, and 20\,\% test data. The final test period was
not used for configuration selection. To maintain unambiguous block context at
case level, 1,693 visits involving more than one RMG block were excluded. The
empirical scope is therefore a defined one-RMG subprocess rather than all
terminal truck movements.

Preserving the ITS schedule-derived stop order stored in XES avoids a
timestamp-induced parallelism artefact, but the raw Inductive-Miner model
still requires an explicit domain repair to enforce sequential truck semantics
and remove a gate-only bypass.  The resulting expert-repaired sequential Petri
net fits all 17,892 held-out visits (fitness 1.0) with a test precision of
0.7641.  This is the single executable model used by every ProSiT
configuration and reported throughout the thesis.

A $2\times2$ validation experiment separates manually constructed static
demand and utilisation proxies from ProSiT's native workload and queue state.
No added-state configuration shows a resolved improvement across turnaround,
frequency-weighted yard-service, activity-rate, and NGD ($N=3$) errors. The
parsimonious \textit{visit-only} configuration is consequently rediscovered on
the complete 80\,\% development period and evaluated once on the untouched
test period. It reproduces inter-arrival spacing closely and parts of yard
service timing, but its mean turnaround is 33.812 rather than 39.742 minutes
and its P90 is 57.900 rather than 71.000 minutes. The narrow Monte-Carlo
intervals show that this deficit is systematic rather than simulation noise.

Two expert-supported physical requirements are audited separately. Activities
of one truck must remain sequential, and no more than three RMGs may operate in
one block. The latter ceiling is imposed transparently because overlap-derived
capacities exceed the physical maximum. For what-if analysis, a purpose-specific
\textit{native-only} model supplies the endogenous queue state absent from the
historical reference. Closing block T22 and increasing working-time demand by
20\,\% produce no resolved mean-turnaround effect. A matched six-level stress
test nevertheless reveals a non-linear response at higher realised arrival
rates. This demonstrates demand propagation inside the fitted simulator, not a
validated physical congestion threshold.

The thesis contributes a layered validity audit that separates event order,
control-flow language, historical fidelity, temporal transfer, physical
semantics, and intervention response. The resulting model is useful for
transparent historical analysis and diagnostic what-if experiments. Direct
prescriptive use still requires explicit location, movement, equipment, and
queue mechanisms together with prospective operational validation.
