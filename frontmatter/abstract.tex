Container terminals use simulation to evaluate operational changes without
disrupting live operations, but these models are commonly constructed and
calibrated by hand. Data-aware process simulation instead derives executable
models from event logs; evidence from physical logistics remains limited.

This thesis evaluates the approach for truck operations at HHLA Container
Terminal Burchardkai. Two months of data were transformed into an event log of
89,460 visits and divided temporally into discovery and held-out periods. The
white-box ProSiT framework was evaluated through a $2\times2$ comparison of
manual demand/utilisation proxies and ProSiT's native workload/queue state,
with visit, process and location state held common. An apparent parallelism defect was traced to timestamp-based event
reordering. Preserving explicit XES order produced a compact sequential Petri
net used as the common executable control flow.

The net fits 99.994\,\% of 17,892 held-out visits. A separate repair removes a
silent gate-only bypass, raising precision from 0.7558 to 0.7939 while lowering
generalisation from 0.9413 to 0.8859. The selected \textit{visit-only}
reference model reproduces
arrival spacing closely and parts of yard service timing, but mean turnaround
is 7.63 minutes too short and P90 is 16.80 minutes too short. The 7.63-minute
test-period gap decomposes into a 2.45-minute increase in the observed mean
between periods and a 5.18-minute simulation deficit relative to the
training-period mean. Paired ten-seed comparisons show that static workload
proxies worsen activity-frequency and 3-gram fidelity, while native state
increases turnaround distance and has no resolved service-time benefit. A
cleaned static sensitivity retains no demand or utilisation split and reaches
the same negative control-flow conclusion. The parsimonious visit-only bundle
is therefore retained as the within-study reference. Because the
same later period informs both this comparison and the reported reference
performance, it is not an independent prospective validation. The
automatically discovered RMG concurrency values
required a documented correction to the terminal-expert-validated maximum of
three cranes per block. Two reproducible policy experiments produce no
resolved mean-turnaround response. T22 removal increases model-derived RMG
pre-service delay slightly, whereas the 20\,\% demand increase produces no
resolved handling mean effect and leaves the
most-loaded held-out RMG block at 0.506 nominal mean utilisation. An earlier
fully contextualised six-level demand ladder resolves the mechanistic
ambiguity: at a realised elapsed-rate
ratio of 2.367, mean turnaround rises by 4.644 minutes and mean RMG pre-service
by 5.878 minutes. That model therefore propagates abstract capacity pressure
near the nominal saturation region, but it lacks the validated spatial
mechanisms needed for physical counterfactual claims.
Repeating the scenarios with the rejected \textit{both} bundle preserves the
mean-turnaround conclusion but reveals bundle-sensitive P90 and receive
pre-service responses. An earlier fully contextualised capped-versus-uncapped
diagnostic produces no resolved direct or cap-by-scenario mean-turnaround
effect; it supports the ceiling as a necessary domain correction without
constituting an uncapped validation of the selected reference.

The thesis contributes a layered validity audit separating event semantics,
control-flow language, historical fidelity, temporal transfer, physical
resource--space semantics, and scenario response. Data-aware simulation is
valuable here as an auditable historical and diagnostic model, but direct
operational decision support requires validated location, movement, resource,
and queueing mechanisms. With these extensions, the approach could compare
truck-slot rates and handling priorities under forecast terminal conditions
before their implementation.
