Container terminals use simulation to assess operational changes before they are
made. Data-aware process simulation learns such models from event logs, with decisions and
durations that depend on the attributes of a case and the state of the process, but
it has rarely been applied in logistics, where queues and shared resources
dominate. This thesis examines whether a simulation learned with ProSiT from the
operational data of HHLA Container Terminal Burchardkai can support the assessment
of a load-aware allocation of truck deliveries to yard blocks. The study uses
91,153 truck visits recorded in March and April 2026, split in time into a training
log of six weeks and a test log of the following 18 days. The terminal aims at
an hourly mean turnaround time between 25 and 35 minutes, a corridor that the mean
exceeds in more than half of the hours of the test period.

Nineteen Petri nets, discovered with four families of algorithms, were compared with
criteria fixed on the training log. Of the three nets that are sound and accept every
frequent sequence, two generate visits that no truck can perform. The coverage of the
frequent sequences and an audit against the domain rules anticipated these failures.
Precision did not. The selected Heuristics Miner net,
used as discovered, reproduces the stop structure once a guard on the planned number
of stops, a data attribute of the case, is added.

As fitted, the simulation overestimates the turnaround time strongly, because its
learned block choice concentrates the trucks on a single block. An analysis along the
path of a truck separates defects of ProSiT 1.0.4, a mismatch between its resource
assumptions and a block with three lanes, missing data on the work of the cranes and a
test period in which the stops took longer. With the first two corrected and the
observed trucks and blocks replayed, the model reproduces the turnaround time of
normal hours and the rise of the waiting with the load of a block. It
produces, however, less than half of the hours above the corridor, and how strongly
it congests depends on the depth of the learned trees.

In this model, moving deliveries at the entry gate away from blocks that three trucks
are already heading to lowers how often the hourly mean turnaround time exceeds
35 minutes in every scenario, through less waiting at full blocks. With 20\,\% more
trucks, the change in the severity of these hours becomes uncertain, a wider choice of
blocks moves waiting to the trucks at the receiving blocks, and a rule restricted to neighbouring blocks is a promising candidate
rather than an established best rule. The terminal confirmed the domain rules and the
decision point of the allocation\rev{, and it found the reproduced behaviour and the
direction of the effect plausible}. The result is a set of hypotheses for a pilot at the terminal, not a validated
improvement.
