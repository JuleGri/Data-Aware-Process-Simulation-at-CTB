Container terminals use simulation to evaluate operational changes without
disrupting live operations, but conventional models are commonly constructed
and calibrated manually. Data-aware process simulation instead derives
executable models from event logs. Its validity in physical logistics is still under-evaluated.

This thesis examines ProSiT as a data-aware Process Simulation framework for truck operations at HHLA Container Terminal
Burchardkai. Two months of operational data were transformed into an event log
of 89,460 truck visits and divided chronologically into discovery, validation and untouched test datasets. 
Preserving the ITS schedule-derived stop order prevents a timestamp-induced
parallelism artefact, but the raw Inductive-Miner model still violates known
truck semantics through parallel behavior and a gate-only bypass. A transparent
domain repair therefore enforces sequential yard handling while retaining
alternative orders and repetitions. The resulting Petri net fits all 17,892
held-out visits with fitness 1.0 and test precision 0.7641 and serves as the
common executable control-flow model.

A $2\times2$ validation experiment separates manually constructed static
demand and utilisation proxies from ProSiT's native workload and queue state.
No added-state configuration provides a resolved multi-perspective improvement.
The parsimonious \textit{visit-only} configuration is therefore rediscovered
on the complete 80\,\% development period and evaluated once on the final
test period. It reproduces inter-arrival spacing closely and parts of yard
service timing, but underestimates mean turnaround by 5.930 minutes
(33.812 vs.\ 39.742) and P90 by 13.100 minutes (57.900 vs.\ 71.000).
The discrepancy is stable across Monte-Carlo seeds.
Physical validity is audited separately. Truck activities must remain
sequential and no more than three RMGs may operate concurrently in one block;
the latter upper bound is imposed transparently because overlap-derived capacities
exceed the physical maximum. Historical fidelity alone selects the
workload-blind \textit{visit-only} benchmark, which lacks an endogenous pathway
for demand propagation. A mechanism-focused audit therefore evaluates the
minimal \textit{native-only} configuration with a dynamic workload state.
Closing block T22 and increasing working-time demand by 20\,\% produce no
resolved mean-turnaround effect, while a matched six-level stress test produces
a non-linear delay response, only at substantially higher realised arrival
rates. This reflects an internal response pathway in the fitted simulator,
not a validated physical CTB congestion response.
The thesis contributes a layered validity audit, separating event order,
control-flow language, historical fidelity, physical semantics, and
intervention response. The case shows that good historical fit is an important first step, but is not enough to show that the model is reliable for what-if analysis. For direct
decision support, the model would need a more explicit representation of
location, movement, equipment, and queues, as well as further validation with
future operational data.
